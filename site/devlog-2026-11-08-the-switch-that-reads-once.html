The Switch That Reads Once

Why some Unreal Engine settings refuse to change while the editor is running, what that taught us while planting a test forest, and how COGHEIM now handles startup only switches.

Published8 November 2026
CategoryEngine & Tooling
SeriesDev Brief

Header image: A test grove in one of COGHEIM's regions, seen in the editor after a restart with the new foliage setting. Work in progress.

This week we planted a test forest in one of COGHEIM's regions, and the first trees arrived bare. Trunks stood in the sand with no leaves on them. The engine was polite about it: it wrote a warning for every tree, telling us the feature these trees depend on was not enabled for the project. The interesting part is what happened when we tried to enable it.

Trees made of parts

The trees we were testing use a newer Unreal Engine system called Nanite Foliage. Epic labels it Experimental in Unreal Engine 5.8 and says to use caution when shipping with it, so we treat it as a candidate, not a commitment. It is worth understanding because it changes how a forest is built.

Instead of storing every leaf and twig as one huge mesh, a tree becomes an assembly: a handful of detailed parts, such as a branch or a cluster of leaves, repeated thousands of times inside the tree. Far away, where leaves are smaller than a pixel, the engine can switch the tree to tiny voxels that keep its silhouette and colour instead of thinning it into sticks. Wind can come from a skeleton of bones rather than from a material that pushes every vertex around. Epic's documentation describes the payoff as a large drop in disk size and streaming memory per tree.

For a game set on a flat disc, where you look out from the deck of a walking city across a long horizon, distant trees that still read as trees are exactly what we want. So the warnings mattered.

The switch that said no

The warning named the setting. The natural move in a live editor session is to type the setting into the console and turn it on. We did, through one of our editor scripts, and the engine answered with a single line: the setting is read only.

That is not a bug. It is a deliberate design, and it shows up in two layers when you read the engine source.

The first layer is the setting itself. The three switches involved, one for Nanite Foliage as a whole, one for assemblies and one for voxels, are all registered with a read only flag and a default of off. A read only console variable can be given a value by the project's configuration files when the engine starts. After that, commands cannot change it.

The second layer is subtler. The small functions that the rest of the engine calls to ask "are assemblies supported?" and "are voxels supported?" look the setting up the first time they are asked, and then keep that answer for the life of the process. Even if the value could change later, those functions would never notice.

Why build it this way? Because decisions like these ripple through everything that comes after them. Whether assemblies are on decides how a tree's mesh is built and cached. In the engine source, the key that identifies a cached build of one of these trees only includes the assembly parts when the feature is on. Whether voxels are on decides which rendering paths exist at all. If you could flip the switch halfway through a session, half of the world would have been built under one rule and half under the other, and nothing would tell you which was which. Reading the switch once, at startup, keeps the whole world consistent.

The project setting in the editor menu agrees with this. It is labelled Nanite Foliage (Experimental) and is marked as needing a restart.

The fix, and what it proves

The fix was one line in the project's engine configuration, followed by a restart. Then came the step we now treat as part of the fix: we checked the new session's startup log for the line that says the engine applied the value. It was there, and it has been there in every session since.

After the restart the trees rebuilt, and the warnings stopped: none in four editor sessions since. Our captures from before and after show the same tree, bare in the first capture and in leaf a few hours later. The hero image at the top of this post is from that second set.

Here is what that does not prove yet. We have not measured what this forest costs on the graphics card at our target resolution. We have not run it in a packaged build, outside the editor. We have not looked at it from a moving city, which is the view that matters most to us. The engine has also told us, on screen, that its lighting cache is over budget in the new forest; that is the next measurement, not a solved problem.

Three rules we took from it

Startup switches belong in configuration, never in commands. Our editor work is heavily automated, and a script that tries to flip a read only setting does nothing except write one error line. Any setting that is read once now goes into the configuration file, with a restart planned for it.

Check the value in the log, not in the menu. A menu shows what you asked for. The startup log shows what the engine actually applied. When we verify a setting, we quote the log line.

Experimental does not go on the critical path without a fallback. Nanite Foliage may become a big part of how COGHEIM's regions look, but our first playable region cannot depend on an Experimental system alone. The fallback is ordinary foliage built without assemblies or voxels. If the new system costs too much or changes under us, the region still ships with trees.

Also this week

Epic has said the Unreal Engine 5.9 Preview arrives this week, with web as a target platform, AI workflow changes, and rendering and animation updates. We will not move our build machine to a preview. We will read its release notes for the fixes we have been waiting on and report what we find.

Separately, users on the current 5.8 hotfix have reported two new problems with systems our editor happens to load, one around character customisation tools and one around AI state trees. We are still on an earlier 5.8 hotfix, so they do not affect us today, but both are now on the checklist for the day we upgrade.

That is the week: a forest that would not grow, a switch that would not move, and a habit worth keeping. Read the log line.
